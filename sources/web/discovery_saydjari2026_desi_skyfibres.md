## What the paper maps (verbatim excerpts)

Abstract: "Using 10.8 million "blank" sky spectra from the DESI Year 3 dataset, we measure the diffuse galactic light (DGL) spectrum in the optical at spectral resolution R ∼ 4000 by correlating with far-infrared emission from IRAS. Subdividing the sky into 54 deg2 pixels (HEALPix, NSIDE = 8), we map the variation of the DGL correlation spectrum and nebular emission lines across the DESI footprint in the high-Galactic-latitude sky."

Sect. 4.1: "We divide the data using a HEALPix (equal area, isolatitude, hierarchical) grid with a resolution of NSIDE = 8 (approximately 7.3° wide pixels)." ... "The spatial maps of these fitted coefficients for two representative lines, Hα and [S II] λ6718, are shown in Figure 5."

Sect. 4.1: "Ideally, these line ratios would probe different ionization states like classic "BPT" (J. A. Baldwin et al. 1981) diagrams ([O III] λ5007/Hβ vs. [N II] λ6583/Hα) so they can be used to trace variations in the "hardness" of the source. However all of our doubly ionized lines are low signal to noise such that we can only report such ratios for the all-sky spectrum. We leave mapping these lines to future work, with larger sample sizes."

## Relevance to the Silk Nebula

The paper measures only the dust-correlated (IRAS 100 µm-correlated) part of the sky-fibre spectra, in ~54 deg² pixels, and maps only Hα and [S II] 6718. A thin [O III] filament that does not correlate with dust would not appear in these products, even if DESI sky fibres sampled it. The paper therefore neither confirms nor refutes the hearsay that S. Koposov found the Silk in DESI data. The way to test it is to use the raw DESI DR1 sky-fibre spectra in the Silk box, which is the DESI teammate's task.
